Fat Loss

The Science of Fat Loss: What Actually Works

By Coach Amar 28 May 2026 7 min read

Every January, gyms across India fill up with people chasing the same goal — and by March, most of them are gone. Not because fat loss is impossible, but because almost everything popular about it is wrong. Detox teas, keto-this, intermittent-that, 1,000-calorie meal plans copied from an influencer who has never seen your blood report. After 10 years and 130+ transformations, here is what actually moves the needle.

Energy Balance Still Wins

Strip away every trend and one principle remains: to lose fat, you must consistently consume less energy than you spend. Every diet that has ever worked — low carb, low fat, fasting, vegetarian, non-vegetarian — worked because it created a calorie deficit. The diet style is just the delivery mechanism; the deficit is the medicine.

The mistake is treating "less" as "as little as possible." A moderate deficit of roughly 300 to 500 calories per day is the sweet spot for most people: fast enough to see weekly change, gentle enough to protect your muscle, your hormones and your sanity. Anything more aggressive usually buys you two fast weeks and then a long, miserable plateau.

Why Crash Diets Keep Failing You

When you slash food intake dramatically, your body does not politely cooperate — it adapts. Daily movement quietly drops, you fidget less, training output falls, and hunger hormones climb. This metabolic adaptation is why the person eating almost nothing somehow stops losing weight, then rebounds harder than before. If you have lived that cycle, the problem was never your willpower. It was the method.

You do not lose fat by eating as little as possible. You lose it by eating as well as possible — consistently, for long enough.

The Four Levers That Actually Move Fat

Once the deficit is set, these four levers decide whether the weight you lose is fat — or muscle, water and motivation:

  • Protein at every meal. Around 1.6 to 2 g per kg of body weight keeps you full, protects muscle and quietly burns more calories through digestion than carbs or fats do.
  • Strength training, 3 to 4 times a week. Lifting tells your body the muscle is needed, so the deficit pulls from fat instead. This is the difference between looking "smaller" and looking transformed.
  • Daily movement (NEAT). Steps, stairs, chores. A simple 8,000 to 10,000 step habit often out-burns an hour of cardio — without the appetite spike.
  • Sleep, 7 to 9 hours. Short sleep raises hunger, drains training quality and pushes you toward the biscuit tin. It is the most underrated fat-loss tool in existence.

So Where Does Cardio Fit?

Cardio is a useful tool, not a punishment for eating. Use it to add a controlled amount of extra burn and to keep your heart strong — two or three sessions a week is plenty for most goals. If your plan is built on hours of daily cardio, it is built on something you will eventually quit.

Yes, You Can Do This On Indian Food

You do not need broccoli and boiled chicken to lose fat in India. Dal, roti, rice, sabzi, curd, paneer, eggs, fish — all of it fits. What changes is the engineering: anchor every plate with a protein source, manage portions of the calorie-dense items (oil, sweets, fried snacks), and plan your social meals instead of "starting again Monday." A good plan has your biryani scheduled in it, not banned from it.

The Finish Decides Whether It Lasts

Here is the part almost nobody does: ending the diet properly. After weeks in a deficit, jumping straight back to old eating is how results evaporate. A structured reverse diet — gradually raising calories over several weeks while training continues — lets your metabolism come back up while your new physique stays. It is built into every programme we run, because a transformation that disappears by Diwali was never a transformation.

Fat loss is not a mystery and it is not a war. It is a system: a sensible deficit, protein, lifting, movement, sleep — reviewed and adjusted every week as your body changes. Run that system for 100 days and the mirror does the talking. If you want it engineered for your body, your schedule and your food, that is exactly what we do.
